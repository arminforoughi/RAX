RAX · Field note 01

Seeing hands

A robot arm that picks lab tubes using one camera on its wrist. No training data, no hand-placed coordinates: it looks, reasons about geometry, moves a little, and looks again.

A1The idea

Most learned robot policies map pixels straight to motor commands. RAX splits the job the way a person would. The camera answers where and which way. The robot's own kinematic model answers how. Every move is small, and after every move the camera checks whether it worked.

A2Key technical parts

01 · DETECT

Measured, not guessed

Caps are found by colour windows measured on the actual camera, each with a test pinning why it sits where it does: the rack's metal, the gripper's tint and the wood grain are all proven out.

02 · LOCATE

Pixel to world, through the arm

The camera rides the wrist, so its pose is always known from forward kinematics. A pixel becomes a ray, the ray meets the table: the tube's position in robot coordinates, from one image.

03 · ORIENT

Read the body, not the cap

Rays cast out from the cap are scored by contrast against their neighbours; the tube's body is the streak that stands out. The wrist turns so the jaws close across it, then re-checks that the picture rotated as predicted.

04 · SOLVE

Vision steers, IK drives

Image error steers only what the camera can judge; the kinematic model solves every joint. A multi-start solver finds a grasp square to the table out to ~30 cm, and the last centimetres are a straight vertical drop.

Self-calibrating. Before correcting, the arm makes a small test move and measures how the target shifts in the image. Directions and gains are measured on the rig, never assumed: on this arm the base turns opposite to intuition, which a guessed sign would get wrong.

A3Beyond tubes

Nothing in the loop is specific to tubes. Swap the detector and the same pipeline, detect → locate → orient → solve → verify, picks cubes, pens or vials, and places into racks, trays or bins. The same visual servo already drives both halves of the task here: bringing a cap between the jaws and bringing a held tube over a hole are one problem seen twice.

A4A data engine, for free

Every attempt is recorded as an episode: joint states, camera frames before and after, and an outcome the robot labels itself: placed, missed hole, missed grasp, not found. 672 tube episodes are on disk from development alone. That is exactly the data a learned policy needs, collected autonomously and sorted by what went wrong, so the precise controller can train its own successor.

A5Any arm

An arm is described as data: its URDF, its camera mount and which joint does what. The servo needs just four things from an arm. The same stack runs on the SO-101 and in simulation, and an X250 is wired to the same controller. Porting to a new arm is a profile file, not a rewrite.

A6Status

Finding, approaching, squaring up and grasping work on the bench. Dropping into a rack hole is the open problem: it depends on an overhead camera calibration that is still rough. Colour detection is tuned per setup today; a learned detector is the natural next step.
